# Map landscape

The map is drawn as one illustrated landscape in every stage (Discover, Find, Travel, Results) and in the close-up:
- a bright atlas surface: turquoise water, green land, white borders, soft painted texture;
- painted relief made from real elevation data, across the whole map: high mountains, lower ranges and rolling uplands where they really are, with lowlands left flat;
- forests painted from real land-cover data (ESA WorldCover tree cover): irregular areas of deeper green with canopy texture;
- in Discover only: a few wave marks at sea.

There are no mountain, hill or tree symbols; the painted landscape replaces them.

Code:
- surface: `src/components/map/AtlasSurface.tsx`, tokens in `.atlas` in `src/components/map/RegionMap.module.css`;
- relief and forests: `src/components/map/Relief.tsx`; data in `src/assets/map/relief.json`, `src/assets/map/relief/` and `public/relief/`, all made by `scripts/generate-relief.mjs`;
- waves: `src/components/map/Scenery.tsx`, artwork in `sceneryArt.ts`, anchors in `src/geo/terrain.ts`;
- sources credit: `src/components/map/AboutMap.tsx` ("About the map");
- tests: `src/geo/relief.test.ts`, `src/geo/terrain.test.ts`, `e2e/relief.spec.ts`, `e2e/scenery.spec.ts`.

## Atlas surface

- **Colours.**
  - Water is turquoise, with a light shallow-water edge along every coast (a wide pale stroke under the land).
  - Land is fresh green with clean white borders.
  - Neighbouring countries belong to the same green landscape, a little paler and with softer borders.
  - A selected country is teal: greener and deeper than the sea. The other state colours are unchanged: teal for Find's correct answer and visited Travel countries, deep teal for the traveller's current country, pale coral for a wrong answer, amber for the destination and Find's last hint.
- **Texture.**
  - Two small tileable WebP textures (`src/assets/map/`, about 29 KB together), generated by `scripts/generate-map-textures.mjs` from periodic value noise: soft light and deep patches for the land, faint streaks for the water.
  - They are decorative colour variation only, not elevation or land cover.
  - They are SVG patterns in world coordinates, so they stay anchored while zooming and panning. Land texture is filled into the country paths themselves; over a country in a state colour it is at 35%.

## Relief

### What it looks like

- **Colour follows height.** Green foothills fade into the surrounding land, then olive and grey-green middle slopes, muted grey rock, and snow only on the highest massifs (most visibly Mont Blanc and the Valais Alps).
- **One light, from the north-west** (upper left), lights every slope, so ridges and valleys read as height.
- **Ranges and uplands.** The Alps and Pyrenees read as mountains. The Massif Central, Vosges, Jura, Black Forest, Ardennes, Eifel, Harz, the Scottish Highlands, the Iberian ranges, the Apennines and the Carpathians show as lower ranges and rolling uplands.
- **Lowlands stay flat.** The Paris basin, the Low Countries, the North German Plain and most of southern England have no painted relief.
- **Borders.** Terrain runs across national borders as one landscape. Borders are drawn on top, as separate lines.
- **State colours.** Over a country in a state colour (selected, answered, visited, current, destination, hint), a neutral overlay is drawn instead of the green landscape colours: light, shade, pale rock and snow. The state colour stays and the terrain keeps its shading. Names, the check badge, capitals, the route and the traveller are drawn above.
- **In Find**, the relief never depends on the question: an unanswered question has no state colours, so the relief is exactly the same whichever country is asked (and the same as in Discover). A test compares the relief's markup for two different questions.

### How it is made

`scripts/generate-relief.mjs`, run once; its output is committed.
1. **Elevation.** Terrain Tiles (Terrarium PNGs) are downloaded and cached: zoom 7 for the overview, zoom 8 for the zoomed levels.
2. **Projection.** The elevation is resampled into the map's own projected "world" coordinates, with the same projection as the map. `relief.json` records control points (Mont Blanc, Puy de Sancy, Brocken), and a test checks them against the map's projection.
3. **Smoothing.** The overview keeps only broad ridges and valleys (about 2.4 km smoothing), since a 390px phone shows the Alps about 220px wide. The zoomed levels keep more of the real valleys (about 1.6 km and 1.1 km).
4. **Painting.**
   - Hillshade: light from 315°, 40° high, vertical exaggeration ×5, ×4 and ×3.2 by level.
   - Elevation colour zones, smoothed further so the zones are broad.
5. **Where it shows.**
   - Colour fades in from about 450 m to 1,100 m, but only where the ground is also rough. Roughness is the standard deviation of elevation within about 5 km, from about 60 m to 320 m. So a high plateau (the Spanish Meseta) stays nearly flat and a range is fully painted.
   - Light and shade fade out below about 600 m and are absent below about 120 m. They are a little stronger on gentle slopes, so rolling uplands read.
   - In the high ranges this is the Alpine formula approved in the prototype.

### How it is drawn

`Relief` in `src/components/map/Relief.tsx`:
- **Anchoring.** SVG `<image>`s at fixed world positions, inside the same transformed group as the countries, so they stay anchored while zooming and panning.
- **Dragging and zooming** (`RegionMap.tsx`, `liveView.ts`).
  - **Measured on two machines**, named with each figure below. Their figures are not comparable with each other.
    - *RTX machine*: NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4060, Windows 11. Every browser headed, driven by Playwright. Versions are the ones the browsers reported, not the machine's own browsers'.
      - Firefox figures: Playwright's own Firefox build, not the machine's installed Firefox. It reported version 155.0 (`browser.version()`, 2026-09-29); profiles recorded with it on 2026-10-09 give rv:155.0, build ID 20260902192955.
      - Chromium figures (the memory figures under "Performance"): Playwright's own Chromium build, which reported 153.0.8010.12 in a check run in the same session. The machine's installed Chrome (154.0.8037.58 on 2026-09-29; on 2026-10-09 its user agent gave only "Chrome/154") ran later checks of dragging and tapping, but no figure in this file.
    - *Radeon machine*: AMD Radeon HD 5450. Firefox 144 (hardware WebRender) and Chrome 154. Nothing more about these builds was recorded.
  - At rest the map is drawn with the page, so names get the browser's sharpest text rendering (subpixel smoothing where the system uses it). Promoting the map to a layer of its own at the start of a gesture meant drawing it first, a visible pause on the first move.
  - Instead, a copy of the landscape (sea, countries, textures, relief, wave marks) is moved. In Blink it is kept drawn as a layer of its own: in front of everything, but 0.4% opaque (Chrome doesn't keep fully transparent layers drawn), so it changes no pixel by more than 1/255 and nothing under it leaves the page's drawing. In Firefox it is fully transparent and no layer of its own at rest, so it isn't drawn at all (see "By browser engine"). When the map starts moving (not on a press alone, so taps still reach the country), the copy (a layer now, if it wasn't kept as one) moves behind the names and map controls and shows in place of the map, which is hidden but moves along so taps land on the country shown.
  - The copy has no borders. A separate light layer holds them, moved with the copy and drawn again during a zoom whenever the scale has changed by more than 6%, so borders keep their width instead of growing with the landscape. The soft coastline stays in the copy.
  - The map's shapes are non-scaling (`vector-effect`), but nothing in the copy or the borders layer is: strokes there are drawn at their on-screen width for the view the layer was drawn for (`--inv`, 1/k), so they look the same at rest. Firefox draws a moving layer again during a zoom if it holds non-scaling shapes, even unstroked ones. RTX machine, Firefox 155 (Playwright's build), a wheel zoom of 32 notches, median of 5 runs, frames over 50 ms / 95th-percentile frame: at 1920×1080 with 2× pixels, 40 / 117 ms with them, 21 / 83 ms without, and 35 / 133 ms in the previous version (no copy); at 1366×800, 12 / 67 ms with them, 4 / 50 ms without, and the same 4 / 50 ms in the previous version.
  - New colours (a selection, an answer, a Travel move, Undo, Restart) are drawn into the copy before it shows if the map starts moving right after the change, so it never shows a frame of the old ones.
  - **By browser engine** (`gestureModeFor` in `liveView.ts`, from the user agent, as no feature test can tell): Blink (Chrome, Edge, Android) uses all of the above. Firefox draws the borders in the copy (so they grow with the landscape during a zoom), because moving the separate borders layer cost it more per frame than it saved (RTX machine, Firefox 155, wheel zoom at 1920×1080 as above: 27 frames over 50 ms with the separate layer, against 21 with the borders in the copy). Firefox also leaves the copy undrawn at rest: kept drawn at 0.4%, it slowed the frames after every selection and language switch. Radeon machine, Firefox 144 (hardware WebRender), 1920×953, Level 1 in Discover, two runs each, kept drawn / not drawn: frames over 50 ms after 5 selections 40, 40 / 24, 19; time until frames were smooth again after a language switch 399, 413 / 171, 115 ms (median); frames over 50 ms in 10 drags 67, 65 / 45, 28; the worst frame in the 300 ms after a drag starts was no longer (median about 115 / 75 to 114 ms). Blink keeps it drawn: undrawn at rest, Chrome 154 on the Radeon machine drew the whole copy when it first showed, about 200 ms before the first frame of every drag and slow frames after it, both with the GPU (the Radeon, its blocklist overridden) and without (the blocklisted default); keeping it drawn costs Chrome a redraw of the copy after each selection, done off the main thread. WebKit (Safari, and every browser on iOS) has no copy: the map itself becomes a layer from the press, as before the copy, because the copy made dragging slower in the WebKit build tested. Only the Windows build of WebKit that Playwright ships was tested, not Safari on macOS or iOS.
  - Names, markers and the route follow the map at their usual size. A country's name stays on the same spot of its country; callouts and capital names keep their offset. Cheap checks run on every frame: a name cut by the edge moves back in if it stays on its country, a callout slides along the edge, a capital name tries its marker's other side; otherwise a name is left out (never added) until the map settles and every name is placed again.
  - The copy is drawn again for a new view when it would no longer cover the view (it extends 30% of the map's size past each edge) or would be magnified more than 2×, and otherwise once the browser is idle after a gesture. New colours (a selection, an answer) reach it 300 ms later when idle, so the change itself is drawn first; if the map starts moving before then, at once. The map is drawn again, hidden, when the gesture ends or pauses for 150 ms.
  - Shortly after the gesture (400 ms) the copy goes back in front, nearly transparent (Blink) or fully transparent and no longer a layer (Firefox), and the map shows exactly as before.
  - While a drag is moving the map, the pointer crossing countries doesn't highlight them.
- **Clipping.**
  - Clipped to land, in two parts: countries in their normal colour get the landscape colours, countries in a state colour get the neutral overlay.
  - Drawn after the land texture and before wave marks, borders, the Find answer outline, routes, names and markers.
- **Taps.** No pointer events: taps and gestures reach the country underneath. Country geometry, the projection and hit testing are unchanged.
- **Levels of detail.**
  - **Overview, one per version of a game level:** one image over everything that version's map can show, loaded when the level is opened (`src/assets/map/relief/<version key>-land.webp`; the version key is the level id for a level's first version, with `-r2` and so on for a later one, `versionKey` in `src/core/lessons/index.ts`). Level 6 has two: `baltic-journey` for attempts started on its first version and `baltic-journey-r2` for its current one; an attempt draws the overview of the version it is on, and never fetches the other's. Importing an image only gives its URL, so a level never fetches another level's overview. The neutral overlay's overview (`<version key>-tone.webp`) covers only that version's countries, which are the only ones that take state colours; it is loaded only once one does. Every level uses the same projection (DATA.md, "One projection for every level"), so the images line up with every level's map.
  - **Zoomed levels:** 512px tiles over the area the map can be panned in when zoomed, fetched only for the visible part plus a quarter-screen margin. There is one grid for all game levels, over all their pan areas: France and Germany use the same tiles in Levels 1 and 2. It is anchored where Level 1's grid began, and grows by whole tiles, so adding Level 2 kept Level 1's tiles (below).
  - **Choosing a level:** by screen density (zoom × `devicePixelRatio`): level 2 from 1.4 device pixels per world unit, level 3 from 2.8. A phone and a desktop fetch what their screens can show.
  - **Settled views only.** Tiles are chosen from the view the player stopped at (a gesture ended, or paused for 150 ms), so zoom and pan animations, redraws in the middle of a gesture, and the first layout don't fetch tiles for the views they pass through.
  - **While loading.** Each tile fades in over 250 ms (instantly with reduced motion). Where a level-3 tile is still loading, the level-2 tile under it stands in. The overview is always underneath, so nothing goes blank.
  - **Tile files.** Tiles live in `public/relief/<content hash>/`, and are served with `Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable` (`next.config.ts`). A new generation writes a new folder.
- **Close-up.** It draws the same landscape at its own zoom, so it picks the detailed level for the few tiles around Luxembourg (Level 1; Levels 2 and 3 have no close-up).

## Forests

### What they look like

- **Deeper green where there is real tree cover**, in irregular areas that follow the data: no outlines, circles or tree symbols.
- **Canopy texture.** Lighter crowns and darker gaps vary the green.
  - At the whole-map view this is only a gentle variation, about 5px across.
  - Each finer level adds smaller crowns, down to 2–3px at the deepest zoom.
- **Hills stay readable.** Forest is painted between the elevation colours and the light and shade, so slopes keep their shading through the forest (the Vosges and Black Forest read as wooded hills).
- **Restrained.** At most 30% (whole map), 36% and 40% (zoomed) of a deeper green, so names, country states and routes stay first. At 320px forests show as soft mottling.
- **Across borders.** Forests continue across national borders.
- **Off water.** They follow the land clip like the relief, and WorldCover classes water separately, so lakes carry no forest.
- **State colours.** Within a country in a state colour, the neutral overlay adds only a light dark-teal forest shading (at most 17%), so teal, coral and amber stay clear.
- **In Find**, forests are part of the same raster as the relief, so they are identical whichever country is asked (the same markup test covers them).

### How they are made

Also `scripts/generate-relief.mjs`, into the same rasters and tiles as the relief (no extra requests):
1. **Data.** ESA WorldCover 2021 v200 is 10 m land cover in 3°×3° cloud-optimised GeoTIFFs on AWS.
   - The script reads only each tile's internal overview over HTTP: level 5 (about 320 m per pixel) for the whole-map level and level 2, level 4 (about 160 m) for level 3.
   - Tree cover is class 10: "any geographic area dominated by trees with a cover of 10% or more".
   - Masks are cached in `node_modules/.cache/arimap-worldcover`.
2. **Forest share.** For each output pixel, tree cover is sampled on a 4×4 or 3×3 grid inside the pixel, in the map's projection, giving the share of the pixel under trees.
3. **Simplification by level.**
   - The share is softened: 2 px at the whole-map level, 1 px on the zoomed levels.
   - Forest shows from about 10% share and is full from about 65%.
   - So at the whole-map level (about 1.5 km per pixel) small woods fade into gentle shading and large forests stay. Zoomed in (about 760 m and 470 m per pixel) smaller woods and clearings reappear.
4. **Canopy.** Value noise anchored to world coordinates, so the texture is continuous across tiles, and finer levels add octaves to the same coarse pattern.

## Simplifications and limitations

- **The relief is simplified and artistic.**
  - Real elevation is smoothed (2.4, 1.6 or 1.1 km by level) and its height exaggerated, so small valleys and individual peaks merge.
  - Relief colours follow smoothed elevation: pasture, rock and glaciers are not mapped. "Snow" is simply the highest smoothed ground (from about 2,600–2,850 m), not the real glacier extent.
  - The light direction is a cartographic convention (north-west), not the sun's.
- **Source resolution.** The tiles at zoom 7–8 come from SRTM (about 90 m data, resampled to about 400–800 m per pixel). Finer sources exist at higher tile zooms, but aren't needed at this map's scale.
- **Forests are simplified.**
  - They come from 2021 satellite classification at 10 m, read at 160–320 m, so hedgerows, parks and woods under a few hundred metres disappear, and clear-cut plantations show as gaps. The Landes is patchier than its name suggests.
  - "Tree cover" includes plantations (WorldCover names oil palm and olive trees as examples) with at least 10% canopy. Broadleaf and conifer are not distinguished.
  - WorldCover has known misclassifications; the map inherits them.
  - The canopy texture is decorative (noise), not measured crowns.
  - Outside the area the map can be panned to when zoomed, forests exist only at the whole-map level, like the relief.
- **Lakes** (Geneva, Constance, Garda…) aren't in the map data, so they show as flat land.
- **Soft at the deepest zoom.** At 8× zoom the finest level is softer than the borders and names.
- **Tile edges.** Chrome can't join two translucent images without a hairline. The overview underneath fills it, so no seam is visible. Measured at zoomed views with forests (390px at 3×, 1366px at 1× and 2×), the colour step across a tile edge is on average 1.5–4 larger (sum of R, G and B out of 765) than just beside it. Crops enlarged 4× show no line.
- **Loading.**
  - A newly visible tile fades in over the overview; on a slow connection the sharper area appears tile by tile.
  - A zoom first shows the overview until its tiles arrive.
- **Performance.**
  - Measured in headless Chromium with GPU rasterisation (desktop 1366px; 390px and 320px phone sizes with touch, some with 4× CPU throttling), not on physical phones.
  - Drawing the whole map once takes the GPU about 60 ms at 1366px, mostly for the detailed country outlines (the coastline stroke alone is about 45%). The landscape itself adds little: frame times with it hidden were within run-to-run noise.
  - Compared with the previous version, in which the map became a layer at the start of each gesture (headless Chromium, 1366px): dragging, touch-dragging and wheel zoom run at about 60 fps (95th percentile 16.8 ms). The first move after the map has been idle no longer waits for a layer to be drawn: input to screen about 15–30 ms for a drag and 45–85 ms for a wheel notch (it draws the borders again), against 110–270 ms before.
  - A drag started right after a change (a selection, an answer, a Travel move, Undo, Restart) still waits while the change and the copy's new colours are drawn: first move about 40–240 ms (before: 33–290 ms).
  - A long continuous zoom still draws the copy again each time it reaches 2×. One or two frames over 50 ms were measured per gesture scenario, against 3–35 before.
  - During a zoom, and for 400 ms after it, the landscape is shown magnified (at most 2×), so it is briefly softer; borders (except in Firefox), names and markers keep their size. Then the map shows, drawn for the new view.
  - Memory, against the previous version (RTX machine, see "Measured on two machines"; Playwright's Chromium, headed; Discover with nothing selected, private bytes of all the browser's processes, median of 5; after 40 drags and wheel zooms, then "Show the whole map" and 3 s idle):
    - Desktop 1366×800: at rest 614 MB (before: 627); after the gestures 584 MB (before: 717, as the map promoted during each gesture kept its layer memory). The page's own process: 131 MB (before: 120).
    - Pixel 7 size (412×915, 2.6× pixels, touch): at rest 487 MB (before: 458, +29 MB, mostly GPU memory for the copy); after the gestures 429 MB (before: 486).
    - The copy adds about 240 elements (829 against 587 on desktop) and 1.7 MB of JavaScript heap. Neither grows with repeated gestures: after 40 and 60 gestures back at the same view, the element count is unchanged.
    - Not measured in Firefox or WebKit, or on physical phones.
- **Repository size.** The committed landscape is about 28.4 MB (4.0 MB overviews for eight game levels, 24.4 MB tiles), against about 24.9 MB before Level 8 (3.6 MB overviews, 21.3 MB tiles), about 23.0 MB before Level 7 (3.0 MB overviews, 20.1 MB tiles), about 20.6 MB before Level 6 (2.6 MB overviews, 18.1 MB tiles) and about 17.9 MB before Level 5 (2.0 MB overviews, 15.8 MB tiles), 16 MB for Levels 1 and 2 and 9.5 MB for Level 1 alone.
- **Generator dependency.** Reading WorldCover needs `geotiff` (a dev dependency, used only by the script).
